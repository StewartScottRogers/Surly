=== Timing Out a Slow gRPC Call

==== Problem

Your client sets a deadline on every call. You want a call made slow on purpose and the client giving up at its time limit, and you want to know who gives up first, the client or the server.

==== Discussion

The built-in `surl.test.Echo` service answers a call after `delayMilliseconds` milliseconds, which is how a test makes a call slow on purpose. With a hold-back of 3000 and `-m 1`, `curl` stops waiting after one second, prints no reply, and exits `28`, timed out. That is not a gRPC status: no status arrived to name, so a client sees a timeout and not a failed call (<<grpc-http2>>). `-s` keeps `curl` quiet and `-S` still shows an error, `-d` gives the request as JavaScript Object Notation, and `-o` and `-w "%{time_total}"` send the reply nowhere and print the seconds the call took. The call takes about the limit and not the hold-back; the check is a range, between one and three seconds, because the exact time depends on the machine. A call that answers inside the limit, 200 milliseconds under `-m 10`, is not touched by it and exits `0`.

`curl` also sends `-m` to the server, as the `grpc-timeout` header, the deadline gRPC carries on the wire (<<grpc-http2>>). The Curl port does this for the `grpc` scheme, which upstream curl does not have (<<curl-protocols>>). `surl` enforces a `grpc-timeout` it is sent. So the same call with `-m 1` and nothing else has two timers that end at about the same moment, and either can be first: `curl` exits `28`, or the server answers `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` and `curl` exits `22`. Both have been seen, on different systems. A test that must not depend on the race leaves the header off with `-H "grpc-timeout:"`, a header with nothing after the colon, as the Solution does. The two cases that show it use a second `surl` with `-v`, which logs every event, and `--log-file grpc.log`, which sends the log to that file: its log names `grpc-timeout` in the metadata of the two lines of the call made with `-m`, the lookup of the method by reflection and the call itself, and in none of the call made with the header left off.

The header can also be sent on its own. `-H "grpc-timeout: 300m"` is a deadline of 300 milliseconds (a number and a unit letter, `m` for milliseconds, `S` for seconds), and the server ends a call that outlasts it with `grpc-status 4 (DEADLINE_EXCEEDED): deadline exceeded`. `curl` exits `22`, the exit for every status other than `0`, not `28`, though `-m 10` has not passed. A server deadline is a status the client has to handle; a client timeout is not.

A deadline can pass part way through a stream. `Expand` answers `count` replies, here ten, each held back 300 milliseconds; with `grpc-timeout: 1S` the server ends the stream after about a second with `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`, and the replies sent before it are printed, some but not all ten, before the status and exit `22` (<<grpc-read-a-server-stream>>).

`--allow-anonymous` lets a call with no credentials in, and `surl` writes a `surl: warning:` line on start while it is on (<<surl-readme>>).
