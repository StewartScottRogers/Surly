=== Granting Each User a Share

==== Problem

Two users share a server: one may only read the reports share, the other may write it. You want per-user share rights, with the reader{apos}s upload refused and the writer{apos}s kept.

==== Solution

Name the folder as a share with `--share`, give each person an account, and list who may do what to the share with `--share-access`: `r` for the reader, `rw` for the writer. The writer uploads with `curl -T`:

==== Discussion

`--share reports=reports` serves the folder `reports` as the share `reports`, the first segment of the Server Message Block (SMB) URL path (<<surl-readme>>). Without `--share`, every folder under `--directory` is a share and every account that logs in may use all of them; `--share` gives a share its own folder, and `--share-access` gives it its own rules. `--share-access reports=reader:r,writer:rw` says that `reader` may read and list the share and `writer` may also upload, rename and delete in it. `-T` makes `curl` upload a file instead of downloading one, and `-s` with `-S` hides the progress meter but keeps an error message, which is where the claims read the refusals. `curl` speaks SMB version 1 and logs in with NTLMv1 alone, so `--auth ntlmv1` and an account per person given with `-u` are what let a session start (<<ms-smb>>).

A grant is separate from a login and from `--allow-uploads`. A wrong password is refused at the session and `curl` exits `67`, login denied. A write needs the grant and `--allow-uploads` both: the `rw` account is refused on a server started without it, and `curl` exits `9`, access denied, while the `r` account still reads. `--allow-uploads` is the loosening option here, and the second `surl` is the case without it.

The reader{apos}s upload is refused with exit `9` although uploads are on, and nothing is written; the name reads back as `78`, remote file not found. The writer{apos}s upload is kept, and the reader can read it.

A share with a grant is closed to every account it does not name. A valid account that is not in the list, here `carol`, finds the share missing: `78` for a read and `78` for an upload, not `9`. A share that no grant names stays open to every login, and with `--allow-uploads` on it takes uploads from all of them, so give each share you care about a grant. The member `@everyone` would open a share to every caller, anonymous ones included, and is not used here.

A member can also be a group. `--group editors=writer` names a group of accounts, and `@editors:rw` grants it. The third `surl` shows a member of the group uploading while the account outside it is refused with `9`. A group lets one line change when the team does.
