=== Keeping a Mailbox Off SMTP

==== Problem

One test mailbox must be readable over Internet Message Access Protocol (IMAP) but must never be able to log in to send mail. You want the same password accepted on IMAP and refused on Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP) for that mailbox alone.

==== Solution

Add the mailbox with `;protocols=imap` after its password. The account then logs in over IMAP and nowhere else, and `curl` reads the mail it was sent:

==== Discussion

`--mailbox address:password` adds a mailbox and the account that logs in to it by its whole address; `--mail-domain` is required, and the address must be inside it (<<surl-readme>>). Without more, the account logs in on `smtp`, `imap` and `pop3` and their Transport Layer Security (TLS) twins. A `protocols=` key after the password replaces that list with the schemes it names, each admitting its TLS twin, so `protocols=imap` leaves `imap` and `imaps` and removes every other protocol. Quote the whole argument: the semicolon belongs to the option, not to the shell.

The solution reads the body of message 1 with `curl -u viewer@example.com:viewpass` and `;SECTION=TEXT` in the URL (<<rfc9051>>). A second mailbox, `app`, has no `protocols=`. The recipe sends `hi.eml` from it first, with `curl --crlf -u ... --mail-from ... --mail-rcpt ... -T hi.eml` (<<rfc5321>>), and the message lands in the viewer{apos}s `INBOX` as message 1.

Over SMTP the viewer is refused: `curl` exits 67, login denied. With `-v` the exchange is `AUTH CRAM-MD5`, a challenge and a response, then `535 5.7.8 Authentication credentials invalid`. That is the reply a wrong password gets from an account that may use SMTP, so the refusal does not tell a caller that the account exists and only the protocol is closed. The mailbox is closed to POP3 for the same reason, since `protocols=imap` named nothing else; `app` logs in on SMTP, IMAP and POP3 as before.

Being kept off SMTP is a rule about who may log in, not about who may receive. Mail addressed to `viewer@example.com` is still accepted and stored, and an IMAP `STATUS INBOX (MESSAGES)` counts it. The IMAP password is still checked: a wrong one is refused, exit 67. Nothing here loosens security. The restriction is not a quota or a permission on folders; it is a list of the protocols one account may use.

On the `curl` side, `-u` is the login, `--mail-from` and `--mail-rcpt` the envelope, `-T` the file to upload as the mail and `--crlf` the line ends mail needs; `-s` keeps the progress meter out, `-S` keeps error messages in, `-w` prints a value after the transfer, here `%{response_code}`, the last reply, and `-X` sends an IMAP command in place of the default one.
