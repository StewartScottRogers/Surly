=== Requiring TLS for Mail with STARTTLS

==== Problem

Your local mail server has an account, and you want mail sent and read over an encrypted connection, with the client refusing to carry on if it cannot have one.

==== Solution

Give `surl` a certificate, an account and the mail listen URLs; tell `curl` with `--ssl-reqd` to upgrade each connection with `STARTTLS` and to trust the certificate with `--cacert`:

The checked claims below also run a second `surl` on ports `8086` to `8088`, `surl --allow-plaintext-auth --user alice:s3cret smtp://127.0.0.1:8086/ imap://127.0.0.1:8087/ pop3://127.0.0.1:8088/`, with no certificate and passwords in clear allowed, to show what that costs. You do not need it. Stop `surl` with kbd:[Ctrl+C].

==== Discussion

Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP) sends mail, and Internet Message Access Protocol (IMAP) and Post Office Protocol version 3 (POP3) read it (<<rfc5321>>, <<rfc9051>>, <<rfc1939>>). Each can protect itself with Transport Layer Security (TLS) in two ways (<<rfc8446>>). On `smtp://`, `imap://` and `pop3://` the connection starts in clear and the client upgrades it with a command: `STARTTLS` for SMTP and IMAP (<<rfc3207>>), `STLS` for POP3. On `smtps://`, `imaps://` and `pop3s://` it is TLS from the first byte. Given a certificate, one `surl` serves all six over one mail store, here on ports `8080` to `8085`: what SMTP delivers, IMAP and POP3 read, upgraded or not (<<surl-readme>>).

`--ssl-reqd` makes `curl` send `STARTTLS` or `STLS` and stop if the server does not offer it; the login and the message then go over TLS. `--next` ends the SMTP transfer's options and starts the IMAP one, which needs its own `--ssl-reqd`, `--cacert` and `-u`. Against that second `surl`, which has no certificate and so offers no upgrade, `curl` stops before it logs in with exit `64`, the TLS level it was told to require could not be had. That is the point of `--ssl-reqd`: without it, `curl` would carry on in clear (<<curl-readme>>).

What `surl` offers depends on whether the connection is encrypted, and `-v` shows it. Before the upgrade, SMTP's `EHLO` answer lists `STARTTLS` and `AUTH CRAM-MD5` only; IMAP advertises `STARTTLS LOGINDISABLED AUTH=CRAM-MD5`, `LOGINDISABLED` meaning its `LOGIN` command is off; POP3 lists `STLS` and `SASL CRAM-MD5`. After it, all three add `PLAIN`, `LOGIN`, `OAUTHBEARER` and `XOAUTH2`, IMAP drops `LOGINDISABLED`, and POP3 adds `USER`. `CRAM-MD5` is offered either way because it proves the password without sending it; every mechanism that carries the password or a token itself waits for TLS. So without `--ssl-reqd`, `curl -u` still logs in, with `CRAM-MD5`, though the mail it then reads is not encrypted. `--login-options AUTH=PLAIN` tells `curl` to use only `PLAIN`: without TLS it is not offered, and `curl` exits `67`, login denied, having sent no password; after `STARTTLS` the same login succeeds. A wrong password over TLS is `67` too.

On the `s` schemes there is nothing to upgrade and no `--ssl-reqd` to give: `smtps://` sends, `imaps://` reads the same message back, and `pop3s://` takes a `PLAIN` login from the first byte. A listen URL that is TLS from the first byte needs a certificate the way `https` does: `smtps` with neither `--cert` nor `--self-signed` is refused at start, exit `58`. The plain schemes start without one, and offer no upgrade.

`--cert server.pem` and `--key server.key` are the certificate and key `surl` serves, made by the `openssl` line in setup, and `--cacert server.pem` is `curl` trusting it; without it, `curl` stops with exit `60` (<<https-serve-with-your-own-certificate>>). For a test, `--self-signed` and `curl -k` can stand in for all three (<<https-serve-with-a-self-signed-certificate>>). `--user alice:s3cret` is the account and `-u alice:s3cret` the login. On the sending side, `--mail-from` and `--mail-rcpt` are the envelope sender and recipient, `-T lunch.txt` the message, and `--crlf` turns its line ends into the carriage return and line feed a message is made of.

`--allow-plaintext-auth` on that second `surl` turns the default off: without TLS it offers `PLAIN` and `LOGIN` as well, and `-v` shows what that costs. `curl` sends `AUTHENTICATE PLAIN AGFsaWNlAHMzY3JldA==`, which is `alice` and `s3cret` in base64, readable by anyone who can watch the network. That is why it is not the default; `surl` warns on every start while it is on (<<surl-readme>>). It is for a test against a client that cannot do TLS.
