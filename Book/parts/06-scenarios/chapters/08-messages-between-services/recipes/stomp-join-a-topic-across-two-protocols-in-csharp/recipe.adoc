=== Joining a Topic Across Two Protocols

==== Problem

In a C# test: your old service sends its messages over STOMP and the new one subscribes over MQTT; you want to know which STOMP destination reaches which MQTT topic, and a test that shows a message crossing from one to the other.

==== Solution

Host one `surl` in process that speaks both protocols on one message bus, subscribe over MQTT 5 as the new service, send over STOMP as the old one, and read what the new service received and what the broker kept:

==== Discussion

STOMP, the Simple Text Oriented Messaging Protocol, names a destination with a path (<<stomp12>>); MQTT, the MQ Telemetry Transport, names a topic with levels split by `/` (<<mqtt5>>). `SurlServerOptions` is `surl`{apos}s command line as properties: `ListenUrls` holds a `stomp://` and an `mqtt5://` listener, each on a free port, and `SurlServer.StartAsync` starts them over one message bus, so the STOMP destination `/topic/orders/created` is the MQTT topic `orders/created`, level for level, with nothing to configure (<<surl-readme>>). `BoundUrls` names the two ports in the same order. `AllowAnonymous` lets both services in without an account; it is for a test. `Topics` holds `orders/#;retain`, `--topic` on the command line, which keeps the latest message on every topic under `orders/`.

Both services are one `CurlClient`, which runs requests side by side. The new service is a `CurlRequest` with `HttpMethod.Get` on `mqtt5://host:port/orders/created`: an MQTT 5 subscription stays open, and the `CurlResponse` body is a stream with each message on it as it arrives, read here a line at a time on a task of its own until the test stops it. The old service is a `CurlRequest` with `HttpMethod.Post` on `stomp://host:port/topic/orders/created`, whose `Body`, made with `CurlContent.FromBytes`, is the message it sends (<<curl-readme>>).

A subscription takes a moment to be in place, so the test does not send its orders blind: the old service sends `ping` until the new service has one, ten tries a second apart at most, and only then sends `order 1001` and `order 1002`, waiting ten seconds at most for the second. They arrive in the order sent, behind one or more pings.

The broker{apos}s own record of what crossed is the topic{apos}s retained message. `server.Messages.ReadRetainedAsync("orders/created")` reads it from the store without a client, as a `SurlRetainedMessage` whose `Payload` is `order 1002`. The store can trail the subscriber by a moment, so the test looks for it every 200 milliseconds, ten seconds at most. A STOMP subscriber to the same destination, a `HttpMethod.Get` on it, is not sent the retained message: retained messages are MQTT{apos}s alone, and the program stops waiting after three seconds, its `CancellationToken` cancelled, with nothing sent. The broker has no idle timeout: one would end the new service{apos}s MQTT 5 subscription too, whenever the old service{apos}s sends came more than that far apart.

The map is exact, and the test shows two ways to miss it. A service that sends to `/topic/orders.created`, with a dot where the MQTT topic has a slash, does not reach `orders/created`. A service that sends to `/queue/orders/created` sends to a queue, and no MQTT subscriber is sent what is in it. Nothing tells the old service; only the new service{apos}s silence does, and after ten pings the test gives up and returns 1.
